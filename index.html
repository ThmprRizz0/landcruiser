Land Cruiser Country

Seven generations of Toyota's toughest four-wheel drive, from the canvas-topped 40 to the twin-turbo 300. These are the Cruisers Australians have driven to the Cape, across the Simpson and down every station track in between.

The first Land Cruisers in Australia were working trucks. In 1958 the construction firm Thiess Brothers brought FJ25s in for the Snowy Mountains Hydro-Electric Scheme, where they carried crews and gear through alpine country that broke lesser vehicles.

Word got around. Graziers, miners, surveyors and the Flying Doctor followed, and Australia grew into one of the Land Cruiser's biggest markets anywhere. Six decades on, a Cruiser with a snorkel and a roof rack is as much a part of the outback as the road trains and the red dust.

This page covers the seven series that matter most here. Each one is drawn below in its most familiar Australian form.

  1. 1951Toyota's BJ prototype climbs to the sixth station of Mount Fuji, higher than any vehicle before it.
  2. 1954The BJ is renamed Land Cruiser.
  3. 1958First Land Cruisers arrive in Australia for the Snowy Mountains Scheme.
  4. 1984The 70 Series launches. It is still in production more than 40 years later.
  5. 2021The 300 Series replaces the 200, with a twin-turbo V6 diesel.

Campfire vocabulary

Words you'll hear at the campground

Troopy
The 70 Series Troop Carrier. A tall-roofed wagon that holds a bed, a fridge and a water tank, which is why so many end up as touring campers.
1HZ
The non-turbo 4.2 litre six-cylinder diesel in 80s, 105s and 70s. Slow, simple and known for running well past a million kilometres.
The 105
The workhorse 100 Series. It kept a solid front axle and the 1HZ when the flagship moved to independent front suspension, so tourers sought it out.
Snorkel
A raised air intake up the A-pillar. It pulls cleaner air above the dust and keeps water out of the engine on river crossings.
KDSS
Kinetic Dynamic Suspension System. It stiffens the sway bars on the road and frees them off-road for more wheel travel. Optional on 200 Series.
Sahara
The top-spec wagon, with leather, the extra gadgets and the bigger price. GXL sits below it and is the touring favourite.
